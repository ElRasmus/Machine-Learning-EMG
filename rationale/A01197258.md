One choice our group made that I initially doubted was recording each muscle contraction 
for only two seconds, since I thought that was too little data to train a machine learning
model.
However, after reviewing the literature, I changed my mind. Smith et al. (2011) 
found that the optimal analysis window for EMG classifiers is only about 150–250 ms.
This means a single two-second contraction can be divided into dozens of overlapping
windows, each providing a labeled sample for training.
Therefore, repeating several short contractions with our participant should provide enough 
examples to detect contraction reliably, while keeping each recording 
session short and manageable.

Smith, L. H., Hargrove, L. J., Lock, B. A., & Kuiken, T. A. (2011). Determining the optimal window length for pattern recognition-based myoelectric control: Balancing the competing effects of classification error and controller delay. IEEE Transactions on Neural Systems and Rehabilitation Engineering, 19(2), 186–192.
